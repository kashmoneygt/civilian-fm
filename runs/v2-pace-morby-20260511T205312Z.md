Starting in real estate with $30,000 and facing challenges like poor credit and insufficient funds for a conventional mortgage can be daunting, but there are still several realistic options you can consider:

1. **House Hacking**: Consider purchasing a multi-family property (like a duplex or triplex) where you can live in one unit and rent out the others. This can help cover your mortgage payments. Look for properties in areas where you can use alternative financing options, such as seller financing or lease options.

2. **Seller Financing**: Some sellers may be willing to finance the purchase themselves. This means you could negotiate a deal where you make payments directly to the seller instead of going through a bank. This often requires a smaller down payment and can be more flexible regarding credit.

3. **Partnerships**: Consider partnering with someone who has better credit or more capital. You could contribute your $30,000 as a down payment or for renovations, while your partner handles financing or management.

4. **Real Estate Wholesaling**: This involves finding properties that are undervalued, getting them under contract, and then selling that contract to another buyer for a fee. This requires little to no capital upfront, but you will need to invest time in learning the market and building a network.

5. **Lease Options**: You can negotiate a lease with an option to buy. This allows you to rent a property with the option to purchase it later, giving you time to improve your credit and save more money for a down payment.

6. **Investing in REITs**: If you’re not ready to buy property directly, consider Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs). They allow you to invest in real estate without having to manage properties directly. This can be a good way to start learning about the market.

7. **Look for Down Payment Assistance Programs**: Some local governments and non-profits offer down payment assistance programs for first-time homebuyers, which could help you bridge the gap for a conventional mortgage.

8. **Consider Alternative Financing Options**: Look into hard money lenders or private money lenders. These options often have higher interest rates but can be more lenient with credit scores.

9. **Invest in a Low-Cost Market**: If your local market is too expensive, consider investing in a different area where property prices are lower. This might mean looking at markets in different states or regions.

10. **Networking and Education**: Attend local real estate investment meetings, workshops, or seminars. Networking can lead to potential partnerships, mentorships, and opportunities that you might not find on your own.

Before proceeding with any investment, make sure to conduct thorough research and due diligence. Understand the risks involved and consider consulting with a financial advisor or real estate professional to help guide your decisions.
