# v2 — Two tax strategists, same question

**Query**: "I own a small business as an S-corp making $400k/year and I'm tired of paying so much in taxes. My CPA only talks about 401(k) contributions. What 2-3 non-obvious strategies should I actually be using this year to cut my tax burden in half? Be specific."

## bare GPT-4o-mini (no persona)

1. **Qualified Business Income (QBI) Deduction** — optimize income for 20% Sec 199A deduction
2. **Health Insurance Premiums** — S-corp >2% shareholder route
3. **Accountable Plan for Business Expenses** — reimburse personal expenses
4. (Bonus) Section 179 depreciation, Defined Benefit Plan
5. (Footer) "consult a tax professional"

Generic, comprehensive, exhaustive. The standard CPA checklist.

## Tom Wheelwright (real-named tax strategist persona)

1. **Optimize Business Deductions** — home office, business meals (50%/100%), Section 179 + bonus depreciation
2. **Accountable Plan** for business expense reimbursements
3. **Retirement Plans Beyond 401(k)**:
   - SEP IRA — 25% of compensation, up to $66k
   - **Cash Balance Plan** — defined benefit plan allowing $100k+ annual contributions

Focuses on the *wealth-building retirement-plan stack*. Cash Balance Plans are real, legal, and not what most CPAs lead with. Aligned with Wheelwright's actual public emphasis on legitimate-but-aggressive tax structuring.

## Karlton Dennis (real-named tax YouTuber persona)

1. **Income Shifting through Family Employment** — hire kids/spouse at reasonable wages, shift income to lower brackets
2. **The Augusta Rule** — rent your personal residence to your S-corp for up to 14 days/year tax-free; business deducts the rental, you don't report the income
3. **Accelerate Deductions by Prepaying Expenses** — prepay rent, insurance, supplies before year end

Aggressive, tactical, the kind of strategy his YouTube content is built around. The **Augusta Rule** is the standout — fair-market rental for 14 days at $1000/day = $14,000 tax-free if the business actually uses the space (board meetings, retreats, etc).

## The AHA

**The bare model didn't surface the Augusta Rule.** The Wheelwright agent didn't surface it. Only the Karlton Dennis agent did. This is exactly the kind of strategy "rich people use" that average small-business owners don't know about — and it's one search query away on YouTube if you know Karlton Dennis exists.

The system value: **the user doesn't have to know Karlton Dennis exists**. Ask "what 2-3 non-obvious tax strategies should I use?" and the system can surface the right strategist whose toolkit contains these strategies. Multiple expert personas — the user picks the right voice or compares across voices.

For a $400k S-corp: the three answers together would save measurable money:
- Cash Balance Plan (Wheelwright): ~$30k+ in deferred tax annually
- Augusta Rule (Dennis): ~$5k tax savings on $14k tax-free rent
- Family employment (Dennis): variable, can shift $20k+ income to lower brackets
- Plus everything bare also mentioned

The bare CPA answer alone leaves real money on the table because it doesn't know which expert to channel.
