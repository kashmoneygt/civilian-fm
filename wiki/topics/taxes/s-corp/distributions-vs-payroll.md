# S-Corp Tax Strategy: Minimizing Tax Burden

S-corporations (S-corps) provide a unique tax structure that allows business income, losses, deductions, and credits to pass through to shareholders, avoiding double taxation. This structure enables S-corp owners to strategically balance salaries and distributions to minimize self-employment (SE) and Federal Insurance Contributions Act (FICA) taxes.

## Key facts
- S-corps allow income to pass through to shareholders, who report it on personal tax returns, avoiding double taxation on corporate income [source: irs-gov--businesses-small-businesses-self-employed-s-corporations.md].
- Shareholders of S-corps are generally considered employees, and wages paid are subject to withholding for FICA taxes [source: irs-gov--businesses-small-businesses-self-employed-paying-yourself.md].
- Distributions from an S-corp are not subject to FICA taxes, unlike salaries [source: irs-gov--businesses-small-businesses-self-employed-paying-yourself.md].
- The IRS requires that compensation for S-corp shareholders be "reasonable" based on the services provided [source: irs-gov--businesses-small-businesses-self-employed-paying-yourself.md].

## How it applies to an S-corp owner
- S-corp owners can pay themselves a reasonable salary, which is subject to payroll taxes, while taking additional income as distributions that are not subject to FICA taxes.
- By balancing salary and distributions, S-corp owners can reduce their overall tax burden, as distributions are taxed at the individual income tax rate without additional payroll taxes.
- Careful documentation and justification of salary levels are essential to avoid IRS scrutiny regarding reasonable compensation.

## Limits and gotchas
- The IRS may reclassify distributions as wages if it determines that the salary is unreasonably low, leading to potential penalties and back taxes [source: irs-gov--businesses-small-businesses-self-employed-paying-yourself.md].
- S-corps must adhere to specific eligibility requirements, including having no more than 100 shareholders and only one class of stock [source: irs-gov--businesses-small-businesses-self-employed-s-corporations.md].
- Shareholders must file Form 2553 to elect S-corp status, and compliance with all filing requirements is crucial to maintain this status [source: irs-gov--businesses-small-businesses-self-employed-s-corporations.md].
