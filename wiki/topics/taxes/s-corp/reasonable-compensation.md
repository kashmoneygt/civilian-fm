# S-Corp Reasonable Compensation Rules

S-corp owners must adhere to specific rules regarding compensation to ensure compliance with IRS regulations. This includes taking a reasonable W-2 salary and understanding the implications of distributions versus salary.

## Key facts
- Corporate officers, including S-corp owners, are generally considered employees and must receive wages subject to withholding for their services [source: irs-gov--businesses-small-businesses-self-employed-paying-yourself.md].
- Payments to corporate officers are treated as wages, regardless of their status as shareholders [source: irs-gov--businesses-small-businesses-self-employed-s-corporation-employees-shareholders-a.md].
- The IRS mandates that "wages paid to you as an officer of a corporation should generally be commensurate with your duties" [source: irs-gov--businesses-small-businesses-self-employed-paying-yourself.md].
- Courts have ruled that characterizing compensation as distributions instead of wages does not exempt it from employment taxes [source: irs-gov--businesses-small-businesses-self-employed-s-corporation-employees-shareholders-a.md].
- The IRS may adjust income and expenses if it determines that an officer is underpaid for their services [source: irs-gov--businesses-small-businesses-self-employed-paying-yourself.md].

## How it applies to an S-corp owner
- S-corp owners must take a reasonable salary to avoid IRS scrutiny and potential penalties for under-compensation.
- Distributions taken in lieu of a reasonable salary can lead to reclassification as wages, incurring additional taxes.
- Owners should document their compensation strategy to demonstrate compliance with IRS guidelines.

## Limits and gotchas
- The IRS does not provide a specific formula for determining reasonable compensation; it varies based on the services rendered and industry standards [source: irs-gov--businesses-small-businesses-self-employed-paying-yourself.md].
- Courts have consistently upheld that the intent to limit wages does not exempt owners from paying reasonable compensation [source: irs-gov--businesses-small-businesses-self-employed-s-corporation-employees-shareholders-a.md].
- Failure to classify payments correctly can result in back taxes, penalties, and interest.
