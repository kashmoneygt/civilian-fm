# Solo 401(k) for S-Corp Owners

A Solo 401(k), also known as a one-participant 401(k), is a retirement plan designed for business owners with no employees, or for those who employ their spouse. This plan allows for significant tax-deferred contributions, making it an attractive option for S-Corp owners looking to reduce their tax burden.

## Key facts
- A Solo 401(k) allows contributions as both an employee and an employer.
- Elective deferrals can be made up to “100% of compensation” up to the annual limit for the year.
- For 2023, the elective deferral limit is $22,500, or $30,000 if age 50 or older (catch-up contributions).
- Employer contributions can be made up to “25% of compensation” as defined by the plan.
- Total contributions (employee + employer) cannot exceed $66,000 for 2023, or $73,500 if age 50 or older (including catch-up contributions) [source: irs-gov--retirement-plans-one-participant-401k-plans.md].

## How it applies to an S-Corp owner
- S-Corp owners can maximize their retirement savings by contributing as both an employee and employer.
- Contributions are based on “earned income,” which is net earnings from self-employment after deducting one-half of self-employment tax and contributions for oneself [source: irs-gov--retirement-plans-one-participant-401k-plans.md].
- Owners can avoid nondiscrimination testing as long as there are no common-law employees, simplifying plan administration [source: irs-gov--retirement-plans-one-participant-401k-plans.md].

## Limits and gotchas
- If participating in another employer's 401(k), the elective deferral limit applies across all plans, not just the Solo 401(k) [source: irs-gov--retirement-plans-one-participant-401k-plans.md].
- A Solo 401(k) must file Form 5500-EZ if it has $250,000 or more in assets at year-end; smaller plans may be exempt from this requirement [source: irs-gov--retirement-plans-one-participant-401k-plans.md].
- Contributions must be calculated carefully to avoid exceeding limits, especially for self-employed individuals [source: irs-gov--retirement-plans-one-participant-401k-plans.md].
