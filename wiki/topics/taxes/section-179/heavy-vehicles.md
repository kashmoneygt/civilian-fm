# Section 179 Deduction for Vehicles Over 6,000 lb GVWR

The Section 179 deduction allows small-business owners to deduct the full purchase price of qualifying equipment, including vehicles, from their gross income. This deduction is particularly advantageous for S-corp owners when acquiring vehicles with a gross vehicle weight rating (GVWR) over 6,000 pounds, as they face different limits compared to passenger automobiles.

## Key facts
- **Maximum Deduction**: For tax years beginning in 2025, the maximum Section 179 expense deduction is **$2,500,000**, reduced by the amount by which the cost of Section 179 property placed in service during the tax year exceeds **$4,000,000** [source: irs-gov--publications-p946.md].
- **SUV Limit**: The maximum Section 179 deduction for sport utility vehicles (SUVs) placed in service in 2025 is **$31,300** if the vehicle has a GVWR exceeding 6,000 pounds but not more than 14,000 pounds [source: irs-gov--publications-p946.md].
- **Eligibility**: To qualify for the Section 179 deduction, the vehicle must be used more than **50%** for business purposes and must be acquired by purchase [source: irs-gov--publications-p946.md].
- **Passenger Vehicles**: Vehicles rated at **6,000 pounds or less** are subject to stricter limits on depreciation, with maximum deductions significantly lower than those for heavier vehicles [source: irs-gov--publications-p946.md].

## How it applies to an S-corp owner
- **Tax Savings**: S-corp owners can significantly reduce their taxable income by utilizing the Section 179 deduction for qualifying vehicles, allowing for immediate expensing rather than spreading the cost over several years.
- **Vehicle Selection**: Choosing vehicles with a GVWR over 6,000 pounds can maximize the deduction, as these vehicles are not subject to the same depreciation limits as passenger cars.
- **Recordkeeping**: Maintain detailed records of business use to substantiate the percentage of business use, which is critical for claiming the deduction [source: irs-gov--publications-p946.md].

## Limits and gotchas
- **Business Use Requirement**: If the business use of the vehicle drops to **50% or less**, the excess depreciation must be recaptured as ordinary income [source: irs-gov--publications-p946.md].
- **Trade-in Considerations**: If a vehicle is acquired through a trade-in, only the cash portion of the new vehicle's cost qualifies for the Section 179 deduction [source: irs-gov--publications-p946.md].
- **Recapture Rules**: If the vehicle is sold or converted to personal use, the previously claimed Section 179 deduction may need to be recaptured, impacting future tax liabilities [source: irs-gov--publications-p946.md].
