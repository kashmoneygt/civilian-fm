# Section 179 Deduction for S-Corp Owners: 2025 Tax Strategy

The Section 179 deduction allows small-business owners, including S-corporation owners, to deduct the full purchase price of qualifying equipment and software purchased or financed during the tax year, thereby reducing their taxable income. Understanding the limits, qualifying property, and interaction with bonus depreciation is crucial for effective tax planning.

## Key facts
- **2025 Deduction Limit**: The maximum Section 179 expense deduction is **$2,500,000**. This limit is reduced dollar-for-dollar by the amount of qualifying property placed in service exceeding **$4,000,000** [source: irs-gov--publications-p946.md].
- **SUV Limit**: For sport utility vehicles, the maximum deduction is **$31,300** for vehicles placed in service in 2025 [source: irs-gov--publications-p946.md].
- **Qualifying Property**: Eligible property includes tangible personal property, off-the-shelf computer software, and qualified improvement property. Land and improvements do not qualify [source: irs-gov--publications-p946.md].
- **Bonus Depreciation**: For property placed in service after January 19, 2025, a **100% special depreciation allowance** is available, but you can elect to take a **40% allowance** instead [source: irs-gov--publications-p946.md].

## How it applies to an S-corp owner
- **Taxable Income Limit**: The Section 179 deduction cannot exceed the taxable income from the active conduct of any trade or business during the year. Any disallowed deduction can be carried over to the next year [source: irs-gov--publications-p946.md].
- **Property Use**: Property must be used more than **50%** for business to qualify for the Section 179 deduction. If used for both business and personal purposes, only the business-use portion is deductible [source: irs-gov--publications-p946.md].
- **Election Process**: To elect the Section 179 deduction, complete Part I of Form 4562 and attach it to your tax return for the year the property is placed in service [source: irs-gov--publications-p946.md].

## Limits and gotchas
- **Phase-Out Threshold**: If the total cost of qualifying property placed in service exceeds **$4,000,000**, the deduction limit is reduced [source: irs-gov--publications-p946.md].
- **Recapture Rules**: If the business use of the property drops to **50% or less**, you may need to recapture the deduction as ordinary income [source: irs-gov--publications-p946.md].
- **Leased Property**: Generally, you cannot claim a Section 179 deduction for property you lease to others unless specific conditions are met [source: irs-gov--publications-p946.md].
