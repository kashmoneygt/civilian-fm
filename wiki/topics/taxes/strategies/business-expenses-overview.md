# Deductible Business Expenses for S-Corp Owners

S-corp owners can significantly reduce their tax burden by understanding what qualifies as deductible business expenses. These expenses must be both "ordinary and necessary" for the business to be eligible for deduction.

## Key facts
- **Ordinary expenses** are common and accepted in the business's industry.
- **Necessary expenses** are helpful and appropriate for the business.
- Examples of deductible expenses include rent, utilities, salaries, and supplies.
- Specific to retail, costs such as inventory purchases, advertising, and employee wages are deductible.
- The IRS allows deductions for business-related travel, meals, and entertainment, provided they meet certain criteria.

## How it applies to an S-corp owner
- **Inventory Costs**: For a liquor store, the purchase of inventory is a primary deductible expense, as it is essential for operations.
- **Advertising**: Expenses related to marketing and promoting the liquor store can be deducted, enhancing visibility and sales.
- **Employee Wages**: Salaries paid to employees, including any benefits, are fully deductible, reducing overall taxable income.
- **Utilities and Rent**: Costs for utilities and rent for the business premises are necessary expenses that can be deducted.
- **Business Meals**: Meals directly related to business activities can be deducted at 50% of the cost, provided they meet IRS guidelines.

## Limits and gotchas
- Not all expenses are fully deductible; some may have limitations or specific requirements.
- Personal expenses cannot be deducted, even if they occur in a business context.
- Documentation is crucial; keep detailed records of all expenses to substantiate claims in case of an audit.
- Certain expenses, like meals and entertainment, have specific deduction limits and conditions that must be met.
