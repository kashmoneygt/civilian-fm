# Hiring Family Members in an S-Corp

Small-business S-corp owners can strategically hire family members to potentially lower their tax burden, but the tax implications differ based on the family member's relationship and age.

## Key facts
- Hiring children under 18 in an S-corp results in full FICA taxes being applicable, unlike sole proprietorships or partnerships where children under 18 are exempt from Social Security and Medicare taxes [source: irs-gov--businesses-small-businesses-self-employed-family-help.md].
- Payments for services rendered by children in an S-corp are subject to income tax withholding, Social Security taxes, Medicare taxes, and FUTA taxes, regardless of the child's age [source: irs-gov--businesses-small-businesses-self-employed-family-help.md].
- Spouses can be employed in an S-corp, and their compensation is subject to the same tax rules as any other employee, including income tax withholding and FICA taxes [source: irs-gov--businesses-small-businesses-self-employed-family-help.md].

## How it applies to an S-corp owner
- Employing children in an S-corp does not provide the same FICA tax exemptions as in other business structures, leading to higher payroll taxes for the S-corp owner.
- Spouses working in the business can be compensated, which may allow for income splitting and potential tax savings, but all standard payroll taxes apply.
- The employment of family members can be a strategy for income distribution, but it must comply with IRS regulations to avoid penalties.

## Limits and gotchas
- The full FICA tax burden on children under 18 in an S-corp can negate some of the tax benefits of hiring family members.
- All compensation paid to a spouse is subject to the same tax treatment as any other employee, which may not yield the desired tax advantages if not managed properly.
- Careful documentation and adherence to IRS guidelines are essential to ensure compliance and avoid audits.
