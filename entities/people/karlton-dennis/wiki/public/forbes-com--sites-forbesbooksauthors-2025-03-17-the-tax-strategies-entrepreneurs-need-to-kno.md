# The Tax Strategies Entrepreneurs Need To Know When Selling A Business

By Matthew Chancey ,

Forbes Books Author.

Selling a business is a key moment and may be an event you’ve been working toward for a long time. That said, without proper tax planning, you could lose a significant portion of your profits to taxes. Capital gains taxes, estate taxes, and ordinary income taxes can drastically reduce your final payout if you don’t plan ahead.

Understanding how to avoid taxes when selling a business is key to maximizing your earnings. In the following sections, I’ll spend some time listing what you can think about before you sell a business. When it comes to taxes, the more planning you do ahead of time, the more tax-efficient the sale of your business can be.

## What to Know about Taxes When Selling a Business

Many entrepreneurs focus on growing and managing their businesses, but when it’s time to sell, they often overlook tax implications. Failing to plan for taxes can cost you millions.

A poorly structured sale can trigger a series of tax events that can leave you with far less than you expected.

Here are some of the benefits you can receive from tax planning:

- Minimize tax liability by structuring the deal in the most tax-efficient way.

- Maximize after-tax profits so you can keep more of what you’ve built.

- Ensure compliance with tax laws while taking advantage of legal deductions and credits.

The earlier you start tax planning, the more strategies you’ll have available to protect your wealth.

## Consider Tax Strategies to Plan for an Exit

If you talk to a tax specialist, you’ll be able to consider different options that will impact taxes when you sell your business.

Here is a brief overview of a few to consider:

- 1031 Exchange: If your business includes real estate, a 1031 exchange allows you to defer capital gains taxes by reinvesting the proceeds into a new property. This strategy helps entrepreneurs continue growing their investments while avoiding an immediate tax hit.

- Qualified Small Business Stock: If your business is a C-Corporation, you might qualify for a 100% capital gains tax exclusion under Section 1202, which applies to Qualified Small Business Stock (QSBS). For this strategy, your stock must have been held for at least five years. Also, the company must have had less than $50 million in assets when the stock was issued.

- 1400z Opportunity Zone Investment: With this strategy, you could leverage the benefits under IRC Section 1400z by reinvesting your capital gains into a Qualified Opportunity Fund. This strategy lets you defer taxes on your original gains—with potential reductions if held for five to seven years—and if you maintain the investment for at least 10 years, any additional appreciation could be tax-free. This not only reduces your immediate tax burden but also promotes investment in economically distressed communities.

In addition to these, you’ll find other options to consider. Some of these include planning related to IRC 453 installment sales, charitable planning, and 338h10 / F-reorgs. You can talk to a tax specialist to go over the specifics of the options available and then make an informed decision.

## Start Planning Early to Maximize Profits

Selling a business isn’t just about negotiating the best price—it’s about keeping as much of your profits as possible. Without a proper tax strategy, you could end up losing a significant portion of your earnings to taxes.

If you’re preparing for a business sale, start tax planning as early as possible. A well-structured tax strategy can save millions of dollars and set you up for long-term financial success.
